Hardware platforms and scaling trade-offs

Superconducting, trapped-ion, neutral-atom, photonic, and other qubit platforms make different engineering compromises. Superconducting systems offer fast gates and mature fabrication techniques, while trapped ions provide strong operation fidelity and connectivity at the cost of slower operations and demanding control.

Neutral-atom arrays can rearrange qubits and explore flexible geometries, but scaling requires stable optical control and consistent readout. Photonic approaches offer attractive communication and networking properties, yet deterministic interactions and loss management remain central design questions. No platform metric is meaningful in isolation from the workload and error-correction architecture.

Hardware roadmaps in 2026 are therefore more informative when they publish yield, calibration stability, two-qubit fidelity, measurement fidelity, connectivity, and uptime together. Manufacturing, packaging, cryogenics, lasers, electronics, and control software are part of the computational system rather than peripheral details.
