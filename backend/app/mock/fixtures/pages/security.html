Post-quantum security planning in 2026

Post-quantum security planning addresses the possibility that future quantum computers could weaken widely deployed public-key systems. Organizations do not need a working cryptographically relevant quantum computer to begin: inventories, dependency maps, data-retention policies, and certificate lifetimes already determine how difficult a migration will be.

A practical program identifies where public-key encryption, signatures, key exchange, and embedded device firmware are used. It then tests standardized post-quantum algorithms and hybrid modes in real protocols, measuring message size, handshake time, memory, hardware acceleration, and operational compatibility.

The risk is also temporal. Information collected today may need confidentiality for many years, and large systems can take years to upgrade. Tracking vendor support, cryptographic agility, long-lived secrets, and validated implementation choices creates a measured migration path while quantum hardware and attack estimates continue to evolve.
