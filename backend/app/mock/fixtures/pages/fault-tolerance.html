Fault tolerance and quantum error correction

Fault-tolerant quantum computing depends on encoding logical information across multiple physical qubits and repeatedly detecting errors without measuring away the computation. Surface-code families remain an important reference because their local checks fit several hardware layouts and their thresholds can be studied with established decoding methods.

The practical milestone is not merely a code threshold. A system must reduce logical error as the encoded block grows while maintaining acceptable cycle time, readout quality, connectivity, and classical decoder latency. Leakage, correlated noise, reset errors, and calibration drift can all make an idealized threshold a poor forecast of system performance.

In 2026, credible progress is reported through logical error-rate curves, decoder assumptions, resource estimates, and comparisons with physical qubits. Those measurements make it possible to judge whether additional hardware is buying a longer computation rather than just a larger headline number.
